### F-001 ú Frame ú Popperian Falsification

Proposed by Karl Popper, the premise is that claims are far more easy to demonstrate as false, and the problem of demonstrating the truth of a statement is often impossible in practice. Therefore, scientific exploration should hinge on the falsification of statements rather than the demonstration of truth. In the case of this program, that should be a tenet of the exploration, such that all statements can be defended deductively.

### F-002 ú Frame ú Lakatosian Programme

Imre Lakatos proposed an explanation of historical research programs, an outline of how science progresses in response To Thomas Kuhn's proposals regarding the cycles of paradigm and revolution in science, the Lakatosian description of a scientific program describes a hard core, which are fundamental tenets of the program which cannot be violated and permit the program to survive in its current form. It has a protective belt, which are statements that may perish without the dissolution of the program. This allows for a far greater degree of flexibility than Kuhn's paradigms, where the revocation of an idea is either folded in or triggers the start of a revolutionary cycle where a new paradigm is formed.

In this particular case, Lakatos's principles are used as a guideline for understanding the program. Specifically, he said that a program's health could be measured through problemshift. A program that has that is in a state of degeneration may indicate the possibility of a failure of its hard core, contrary if it is in a state of progression whereby the protective belt is growing. These concepts are borrowed and modified for modernity as a metric by which the automated aspects of the program can be monitored. 

### F-003 ú Result ú Markov Blanket - Pearl

In his 1988 book, Judea Pearl discussed the concept of a statistical Markov blanket. This can be visualized as a set of nodes on a graph. Through that set of nodes, so long as all are known, the states interior to the set can be considered independent of those exterior.

Functionally, this means that as long as you know the states of all nodes in the Markov blanket, the states of the internal nodes are independent of the states of the external nodes, except for those in the Markov blanket. This allows for conditional statistical independence, where a system can be viewed internally as independent so long as the states of the Markov blanket are known.

In the case of this program, this is to allow for delineation between a system and its exterior. A comparison can be made between information that belongs within the system and information that is external to that system, so that inference can be compared to external states predicted by internal transformations. 

### F-004 ú Frame ú Markov Blanket - Friston

In his work on the Free Energy Principle, Karl Friston extended Pearl's concept of the Markov Blanket to a philosophical sense such that it is the mechanism of individuation between entities in the world. He uses this to lay the foundation for entities resisting entropic decay by minimizing variational free energy, which is a statistical metric based on Helmholtz free energy and physics. In the case of this program, the Blankets of individuation are accepted, but their qualification does not. Friston indicates that any system within a Markov blanket that is individuated by a Markov blanket will exhibit behaviors that minimize free energy, or, more intuitively, minimize surprise. 

### F-005 ú Instrument ú Predictive Information

Predictive information is how well a system dynamics can predict the next state of that system and/or features external to that system. Specifically, predictive information is a ratio against a ceiling of possible predictive information, where the ceiling would be the entire external state is predicted by the dynamics of the system. For the case of this project, predictive information is a system with useful dynamics to serve as an inference engine. Evaluating predictive information against a ceiling is a decision of this program to avoid the uncomputability, as described by Legg and Hunter in the 2007 paper, of summing over environments under a Kolmogorov Complexity Weighting. 

### F-006 ú Instrument ú Four Dimensions of Representation - Pohl, Walker

In their 2026 paper and its predecessor, Steven Pohl, Edgar Walker, and several other contemporaries outline four different dimensions by which representations can be quantified. Specifically, they are derived from information-theoretic terms and are:
- sensitivity
- specificity
- invariance
- functionality
Sensitivity is how much a system, or how much a representation, can accurately predict what it purports to represent. Specificity is how much it only predicts what it purports to represent as opposed to additional external features. Invariance is how well its predictive value, or how strong its predictive value is, holds up through transformations of the external system, the system as a whole. Functionality is not actually an information-theory-derived feature, but rather how useful the information and predictions derived from the representation are for the system that forms it. This program borrows these axes of measurement as a useful tool for evaluating the presence and quality of representations. 

### F-007 ú Frame ú Scheme

Among other things, Jean Piaget outlined a concept he calls Scheme, which is a generalizable structure within cognitive systems. He primarily observed this from a psychological perspective. The Scheme, as he describes it, is through which action can be derived and new understandings can be integrated via a process called accommodation, and generalized facts can be processed via a process he calls assimilation. This is a developmental process that largely allows for generalization of facts through the creation of structures that are useful for processing them. The visual analogy often offered to describe Piaget's schemes is toy boxes with holes of different shapes in them, such that blocks can be placed into those holes. Placing a block in is the process of assimilation, and adding a new hole shape is the process of accommodation.  In the case of this project, Scheme maps to my concept of schema, which is a mechanistic interpretation of this same system.

### F-008 ú Instrument ú Schema Metric Spaces

When working with dynamical systems, metric spaces are often selected based on the features being evaluated within the dynamical system, such that the space can be modeled to offer useful predictive value. Dynamical systems can also be decomposed or composed from smaller systems that contain only a subset of the features of the greater system within a unique metric space that allows for tractable solutions to the sub-problem. This could be described analogously via the comparison between a documentation writer and a novelist. The former operates within a very different set of constraints that modulate their end result from the latter, despite the processing or the task itself being the same. Each would have a distinct schema because their considerations for their sub-task of writing are different.

### F-009 ú Instrument ú Cellular Sheaves

Cellular sheaves are a mathematical tool that is applied on top of a graph to allow for heterogeneous nodes. A standard graph presumes that all nodes are identical. A sheaf enables non-identical nodes to be operated upon collectively via stalks and restriction maps. A restriction map transforms the message passed between nodes of the graphs between disparate metric spaces and can be conceptualized in a similar manner to a fiber bundle from physics. In this project, cellular sheaves are a tool that is used to allow the transportation of data between and the interaction of different schemas. 

### F-010 ú Instrument ú Gague Theory/Symmetries

In physics, gauge theory is the tool which allows us to understand how certain forces are invariant across specific transformations and symmetries called gauge symmetries. Gauge here refers to the tool of measurement, and symmetries mean invariance over transformation. In this program, Gauge Theory is used as an analogy and a plausibility warrant for investigating what sort of symmetries might occur in naturally developing systems that could enable heterogeneous inference with a unified output as per the binding problem.

### F-011 ú Instrument ú Laplacian (Connection/Graph/Sheaf)

A Laplacian is a mathematical tool, specifically a second-order differential operator, which allows the evaluation of a local scalar property within a system. A connection Laplacian extends this to vectors, a graph Laplacian extends this to graphs, and a sheaf Laplacian extends it to sheaves over graphs. In this particular case, these are considered the opposite direction and are considered special cases of the more general direction (eg. a Connection Laplacian is a special case of a Sheaf Laplacian). For this program, the sheaf Laplacian is the mathematical tool used to apply the restriction maps of the sheaf to the graph as it is evaluated.

### F-012 ú Frame ú Columnar Organization

Initially proposed by Vernon Mountcastle as an observation on the neocortex, this is a particular perspective on the biological organization of the brain. It posits that there is some universal function that underlies the neocortex, such that it can be partitioned into many columns of roughly 100 neurons in six layers or laminae. This is a fairly foundational assertion from neuroscience, but the degree of importance is still contested.

The concept was expanded upon by Jeff Hawkins in his "Thousand Brains" theory, where he posits that each column acts as an independent predictive engine, transforming sensation to motor action. In this program, Mountcastle's postulate is accepted on the observed premise, but the assertions of specific function from Hawkins' "Thousand-Brain" theory are excluded, but not rejected. Instead, the presumption is that some underlying function repeated recursively can be composed to the whole of observed cognition.

### F-013 ú Result ú Least Action

The principle of least action is a principle mathematically equivalent to the second law of thermodynamics and describes how a acts on a global scale and describes how a system changes over time. It states that objects within a system will travel the path where action is minimized. Action in this case is the Lagrangian integrated over time. The Lagrangian in this case refers to the difference between kinetic and potential energy of the system at any given moment. From this principle, the Newtonian laws of mechanics can be derived, and it is asserted to apply to all systems. In the context of this program that licenses the scale corollary as any Complex systems composed of discrete objects can be treated as a thermodynamic system and thus be considered with the principle of least action.

### F-014 ú Instrument ú Excess Entropy

Excess entropy is a concept that was proposed by Crutchfield and Feldman and refers specifically to the mutual information, which is the information contained by two dependent features between the process's past and its future. Its measured quantity is called entropy rate, and it is a measurement of a system's order.

### F-015 ú Frame ú Edge of Chaos

The edge of chaos refers to a theoretical concept: chaotic systems that exhibit a certain degree of chaos in a mathematical sense, or rather, a certain degree of statistical complexity, also exhibit emergent patterns of order. This has been explored thoroughly via von Neumann's concepts of cellular automata and has been explicitly described and measured in Stephen Wolfram's adaptations in one-dimensional cellular automata, or as he calls them, elementary cellular automata (however, the classifications of ECA are contsted, thus not accepted unequivically here). In this program, we assert that the edge of chaos can serve as a bound to identify dynamical systems which exhibit high levels of predictive value beyond what is explicitly declared.
